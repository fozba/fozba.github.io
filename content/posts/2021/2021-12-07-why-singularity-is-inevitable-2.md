Today, I want to start the post with a scientific paper. It’s an amazing one (and for those who don’t like reading scientific papers, I’ll also add a news article about the study, which will be much easier to read). In this [paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-021-00804-y.pdf) (which has been summarized in this [article](https://futurism.com/scientists-brain-function-implants-ai)), scientists improve the mental function of epilepsy patients by placing numerous electrodes in their brains. These electrodes record brain activity, and the recorded activity is fed to a machine learning algorithm. Then, the algorithm “decodes” the brain activity and detects when a patient is having trouble with mental function. When this happens, the electrodes apply tiny bursts of electrical stimulation to “assist patients in regaining control.”

This is huge. Seriously.

What happens here could be reviewed from many different angles. But why I think this is huge lies within another perspective. But, as usual, before delving deeper, I feel like I have to state that I’m nowhere near being an expert in neuroscience, and what I’m going to write here should be treated as a brainstorm of an enthusiastic amateur.

I think what has been achieved here is actually *constructing a virtual brain part* or at least *virtually augmenting the brain*. When you think of it, there’s a physical attachment to your brain (those tiny electrodes), but what happens is that *there’s an artificial intelligence* within this gadget, which *decides* if you’re having a hard time with cognitive functions and *takes action*. Isn’t that what the brain does on a much more complex scheme? Suppose you’re in a very stressful situation: You encounter a wild animal in a forest. Your brain decides that your body must be in its sharpest state to take the best course of action for survival, and your amygdala sends a distress signal, which causes your hypothalamus to activate the sympathetic nervous system by sending signals through the autonomic nerves to the adrenal glands (I just read these from [here](http://health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/understanding-the-stress-response), thanks Harvard!). In a crude summary, what happens is that one of your brain parts sends impulses that change the state of your body to get you ready for the situation. Isn’t the research about the same thing? The patients have a new, virtual part of their brains. And this virtual part is responsible for maintaining brain activity. *A virtual brain part that maintains brain activity*. Isn’t that amazing?

If you read further, epilepsy treatment is just one aspect of the study, and it’s been used to demonstrate what could *possibly* be achieved with this type of treatment. Because, according to the paper, not only epilepsy but also various disorders like anxiety, addiction, etc., can be treated with the same approach. Boy, oh boy! We’re on the verge of being able to construct virtual brain parts that augment the way our brain works. If this isn’t transhumanism, I don’t know what it is. We’re living in the future, folks!

When I first read this paper, my imagination went crazy. But is it even possible for one’s imagination not to go crazy after reading something like this (especially if you’re a sci-fi enthusiast transhumanist, lol)? Think about what we can achieve with this. Are you addicted to a substance? Well, this “brain assistant” will help you quit your addiction. Philip Morris will be very sad after this technology. But not just addictions—with more and more data, with more and more R&D, we’ll discover much more about brain activity, and our domain of inference and interference will broaden. Imagine you’re on exam week and you want to concentrate more; this gadget will do it for you. Imagine you’re a surgeon, and the next operation will take a very long time. You put on this gadget and maintain your brain activity. The virtual brain will get more and more capable of doing things every day.

At some point, just imagine that the virtual brain becomes more than a mere augmenting agent. Imagine that you can “communicate” with the gadget somehow, and it can do calculations for you. Imagine you have AlphaZero embedded in your brain that can play chess better than any engine. Imagine you don’t have to bring a TI-84 to exams because your “AI assistant” is capable of doing any calculation and any graphing that a calculator can do (of course, it would be pointless to have exams at that point, but you get the idea). It’s sad that everyone thinks of *Terminator* when we talk about the future of AI, but what I imagine as the future of AI is something in this scope, where we discover a way of merging our mind with the machine.

A new era will start when this machine can connect to the internet. Then, there will be no more smartphones. No more “swipes.” Imagine surfing the web “within your mind.” Imagine texting with thoughts. Though, I’m definitely not sure how the scientists and engineers will solve the issue of graphics. Google Glass was really promising in this sense, but when we develop the technology up to this point, I want to believe it will be figured out somehow.

Now, let’s discuss the dark side of the coin. If such a technology becomes mainstream, then, naturally, it will be *commercialized*. These gadgets will do more or less the same stuff our smartphones do right now. And in the future, if these gadgets become a thing, get ready for seeing ads of things you *think about*. Or get ready for rumors like, “Since I’m using X brand Brain Assistant, *I want to eat from Y brand more often.*” It will be a new era in advertising, where ads aren’t retargeting; instead, the ads are *your personality itself*. Sounds dystopic? I disagree. I love retargeting ads, actually. As long as there are no severe privacy issues, it’s more than okay for machines to know me better. If I’m going to see an ad one way or another, I’d want it to be somewhat related to my interests. And there’s nothing wrong with that. Right now, I just realized this section needs its own post and is too long to be discussed in this one. I’ll write something about the possible privacy issues of such a technological advancement in the future. But it won’t be in the scope of the “Why Singularity Is Inevitable” series.

Anyway. My point is, at some point, this “AI assistant” will be so advanced that it will stop being a mere assistant anymore. It will be a genuine, virtual part of our cognitive system and consciousness, which will transcend human intelligence to the next level. Remember the first post? Once upon a time in history, all scholars were polymaths because if you knew even 8th-grade geometry, you were one of the best geometricians of the time. Now, there’s zillions of information, and it’s impossible for one human to meaningfully digest this much of it. But this will change when we introduce AI into the human mind. AI can handle all the storing and retaining parts. Imagine lawyers able to completely memorize the law, physicians able to completely memorize human anatomy, pharmacology, etc. Imagine the boost in our scientific output. Just amazing. We’re actually on the verge of [technological singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_singularity) (the term is coined by von Neumann. What an amazing person, right? Founders of computer science are the greatest cyberpunks of all time). It will all start with Brain-Computer Interfaces, but when it happens, these systems will be so complex that there will be no difference between brains, computers, and interfaces.
