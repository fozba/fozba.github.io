### Yaz!

Since my super-early childhood, I was fascinated with knowledge and experience, and the beauty of it. Maybe because I was spending most of the time at home with my mother, I was always curious about what is happening outside, even when I was around the age 2, which I barely remember. The most shocking event at that time was to see my father looking at a piece of paper one day, only to find out that it is newspaper *(or gazete, in Turkish)*, apparently, an object that you look at and learn what is going on around the world. The idea fascinated me: That you can look at a piece of paper and it tells you what is being experienced around anywhere in the world. Not just in your street, not just in the next street, even not just in your city, EVEN NOT JUST IN YOUR COUNTRY, but in the WHOLE WORLD! Although I barely remember those times, I still remember that fascination, and thinking to myself "I must learn this!". So I started to look at the newspapers, and meanwhile I have also realized that the texts are everywhere around me, not just newspapers. The news, the streets, billboards, back of the chips packages (I still remember the "Did you know these?" section at the back of Ruffles bags)... The information was everwhere for me to learn! But the problem when you are almost 3 years old and try to read something is that, you have no idea how to do it. I didn't even understand the concept of letters at all! For me, there were some short shapes and some long shapes. Some of them had smiling faces inside (Ü), some of them had circles (O), but never triangles, interestingly! As for the squares, some had these things that look like squares, but one side was always wobbly (D). And last but not least, some smiling faces interestingly didn't have eyes (U). That is why, it was so hard in the beginning. But more I looked at the texts, I realized that, some texts were more *frequent* than the others. So I thought to myself two things:

1. The more frequent texts must be experienced more
2. If I learn the more frequent texts first, then I can *unlock* more possible events to learn with the same effort, then learning less frequent ones.

*(These two things I still use to this date, and helps me to learn a new language super-fast, lol)*

Then, I don't remember this part, which I heard from my parents later on, what I have done was very clever for a 3-year old: I pick up a pen and some papers, and approach anyone around me and harass the sh*t out of them about ANY object around that I see frequently, and say:

Me: **Bu ne?** (What is this?)

Any unfortunate adult human being around me: Masa. (Table.)

Me: **Yaz!** (Write (it)!)

So this way, I figured out this insane algorithm that unlocks any text around.

##### Algorithm: Adult Interaction Procedure

1. Find an adult
2. Say "Bu ne?"
3. Wait for adult to reply
4. Say "Yaz!"

With this clever algorithm, I have unlocked many words and by the end of age 3, I was reading Turkish enough to understand what the newspaper says. Huge victory.

But, curious people will know that the curiosity is a never-ending kind of pleasure. One day, knowing that I read stuff, my aunt gave me my cousins' old encyclopedias:

Like many individuals out there who put effort into things and start overcoming challenges at young ages, I had a life with some unique challenges, which, for me was my family life. At this point of writing, I have to say that I am trying to keep this blog not too personal, but when it comes to writing things as they are, I can say that maybe in a world of hardships, learning made me feel connected to the world, and gave me the joy and showed me the beauty of living. That is why, for a bio section, I was very hesitant about what to include and what not to. Like, I don't know what is actually relevant and what is just too much details. But I believe that learning how to read and reading these encyclopedias have a fundamental place in my personality. Because after I read these encyclopedias, I was amazed about how many awesome things are there. I learned about science, humanity, history and many, many things from them. It was not the knowledge that I learned from them, but the joy of discovering what is possible and what kind of things have been experienced by humanity that gave me the first hook on love of learning.

Humans went to the Moon, humans built the radio, humans built ships and discovered the buoyant forces... Crazy, crazy stuff! One crazy thing for me was that all these things being found and experienced, but the real temptation lurking in the convolutions of my brain was the *idea of building these things myself and experiencing it myself*. Just as learning how to read these wiggly-squiggly images called the texts *unlocked* me new opportunities, building these things could do the same, if not more! I greatly remember seeing a picture of Uncle Scrooge McDuck in one of these encyclopedias, getting ready to dive in a pool of gold coins:

What if I build a machine to mine gold? What if I build the ships myself? What if I build the rockets to go to the Moon, and be the astronaut at the same time? All these ideas and daydreaming about them made me very happy, only to increase my motivation to learn more and more. Because, learning how they are built was not enough: I needed to learn how to build them at my house! I am incredibly glad that a child, especially an ever-dreaming one, does not know anything about the word *impossible*!
